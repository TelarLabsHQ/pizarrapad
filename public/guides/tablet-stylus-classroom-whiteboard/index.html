How to Use a Tablet and Stylus as a Classroom Whiteboard

A tablet with a stylus is one of the best whiteboards a classroom can have: you write by hand, face the class, and everyone sees it large on the screen.

What you need

  • A tablet with a stylus: a Samsung Galaxy Tab with S Pen, an iPad with Apple Pencil, a Windows tablet with a pen, or any tablet with a basic stylus.
  • A way to show the tablet on the big screen: a cable adapter, or a TV or streaming device you can cast to.
  • A browser. Open PizarraPad; there is nothing to install and no account.

Step 1: Connect the tablet to the screen

With a cable (most reliable)

  • Android tablets with USB-C (most Galaxy Tab S models): use a USB-C to HDMI adapter. Some Samsung tablets open a desktop-style view (Samsung DeX) when connected; you can use PizarraPad there too, or switch to mirroring from the tablet’s quick settings or notification.
  • iPad: use a USB-C or Lightning to HDMI adapter, depending on your iPad.

Without a cable

  • Android: use Smart View (Samsung) or Cast to a smart TV or a Chromecast on the same Wi-Fi.
  • iPad: use Screen Mirroring to an Apple TV or an AirPlay-compatible TV.

Wireless is convenient, but it can lag a little behind your pen and depends on the Wi-Fi. For full lessons, a cable is calmer.

Step 2: Set up the board

  1. Open PizarraPad and choose a board under ⋯: whiteboard, blackboard or greenboard. On most projectors, the blackboard or greenboard is easiest to read.
  2. Pick a background: Lines for handwriting and language lessons, Grid for math and science.
  3. Turn on Pen only. Now only the stylus writes. Your palm can rest on the screen, and fingers scroll and zoom instead of drawing.
  4. Choose ⋯ → Full screen so the browser bars disappear from the big screen.

Tip: add PizarraPad to your home screen (in Chrome or Samsung Internet, Add to home screen; on iPad, Share → Add to Home Screen). It then opens like an app, and keeps working when the Wi-Fi drops.

Step 3: Write so the back row can read

  • Use the Thick pen. Pressure works with styluses that support it, so press a little harder for bolder lines.
  • Write larger than feels natural. Zoom out with two fingers to see the whole page, and zoom in to write details.
  • Use one or two colors with a purpose: white or yellow for the main content, a second color for examples.
  • Use the highlighter to mark key words, and the laser to point without leaving marks.

Step 4: Use pages like a lesson plan

Add a new page with + for each part of the lesson: warm-up, new idea, worked example, practice. Moving between pages with the arrows is quicker than erasing, and you can go back when a student asks a question.

You can also add a picture with the Image tool, such as a diagram, a map or a photo of a worksheet, and write on top of it. On a tablet, you can take the photo with the camera right there.

Step 5: After class

  • Everything is saved on the tablet automatically.
  • Use ⋯ → Save page as image to share a page with students who were absent, or to keep a record of the lesson.
  • Use Start a new board for the next class, after saving anything you need.

Common problems

ProblemFix
My hand leaves marks.Turn on Pen only.
The picture on the TV is delayed.Use a cable, or move closer to the Wi-Fi router and close other apps.
Notifications pop up on the big screen.Turn on Do Not Disturb before the lesson.
The tablet goes to sleep.Set the screen timeout to 30 minutes or more, and keep it plugged in.
