How to Teach Math on a Projector: Grids, Number Lines and Worked Examples

Math is easier to follow when students watch it being worked out, step by step. A digital board on the projector gives you a clean grid, colors and pages, without dusty chalk or a cramped whiteboard.

Set up the board for math

  • Open PizarraPad and choose the Grid background under ⋯. The grid keeps columns of numbers lined up and makes graphs quick to draw.
  • Use the greenboard or blackboard on bright projectors; dark boards with light chalk colors are easiest to read from the back.
  • Use the Thick pen for numbers, and the Medium pen for small labels.

Worked examples, step by step

Students learn a method by seeing each step and why it happens. On the board:

  1. Write the problem at the top of a fresh page.
  2. Write each step on its own line, lined up on the grid.
  3. Use one color for the working and another for the reason, for example “divide both sides by 3” in a second color beside the step.
  4. Highlight the answer, or draw a box around it with the Shape tool.
  5. Start a new page for the next example. The finished example stays on the previous page if students want to look back.

A useful routine is “I do, we do, you do”: you work the first example, the class tells you each step for the second, and students solve the third on their own while you walk around.

Number lines

  • Draw a straight line with the Shape tool, using the arrow for direction.
  • Use the grid to space the marks evenly. Count squares rather than guessing.
  • Show jumps for addition and subtraction with curved pen strokes, in a bright color, and write the size of each jump above it.
  • For fractions and decimals, zoom in on part of the line and mark the smaller steps.

Graphs and coordinates

  • Draw the x and y axes with the arrow shape, then number them along the grid.
  • Plot points with small dots and label them, like A(2, 3).
  • Join points with straight lines from the Shape tool for linear graphs, or freehand for curves.
  • Use a different color for each line when comparing two graphs, and add a small key.

Geometry

  • Draw boxes and circles with the Shape tool, then label sides and angles.
  • Add a picture of a shape, a floor plan or a real object with the Image tool, and mark measurements on top of it.
  • Use the laser pointer to trace around a shape while you explain perimeter, without leaving marks.

Practice with the whole class

  • Mistake hunt: work a problem with one deliberate error. Students find it and explain the fix.
  • Students at the board: if you use a tablet, pass it to a student to solve a step while the class watches the big screen.
  • Quick check: write three problems on a page, students solve them on paper, then reveal the answers one at a time.

After the lesson

Save the worked examples with ⋯ → Save page as image and share them with the class. Students who were absent, or who want to review before a test, can see every step exactly as you wrote it.
