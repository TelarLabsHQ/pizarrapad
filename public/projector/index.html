Use PizarraPad with a projector or TV

PizarraPad runs in the browser, so it shows on any screen your device can connect to. Pick the setup that matches your classroom.

1. Laptop connected to the projector (easiest)

  1. Connect the laptop with an HDMI or VGA cable.
  2. On Windows press Windows + P and choose Duplicate; on a Mac open Displays and choose Mirror.
  3. Open PizarraPad and choose ⋯ → Full screen.
  4. Write with the mouse, a touchpad, or a cheap drawing tablet, or type.

2. Tablet or phone mirrored to the screen

Write by hand on a tablet while the class watches the big screen.

  • Android (Samsung and others): use Smart View or Cast to a smart TV or a Chromecast; or use a USB-C to HDMI adapter (most Samsung Galaxy Tab S models support this).
  • iPad / iPhone: use Screen Mirroring to an Apple TV or AirPlay TV, or a Lightning/USB-C to HDMI adapter.
  • Windows tablets: press Windows + K to connect to a Miracast display.

3. Interactive board or smart TV with a browser

Open PizarraPad in the board's built-in browser and write with its pen or your finger.

Tips for the back row

  • Use the Thick pen and Large text.
  • On bright projectors, the Blackboard or Greenboard with yellow or white chalk is easiest to read.
  • Hide the tool bar for a clean screen while you explain.
  • Write in the middle of the page. The edges of a projected image are often cut off or blurry, especially on older projectors.
  • Use color with a purpose: one color for the main idea, a second for examples, red only for corrections. Too many colors are harder to read from far away.
  • Pale colors like yellow look great on the blackboard but almost disappear on the whiteboard. Switch boards rather than fighting the projector.

Before the lesson: a 3-minute check

  1. Open PizarraPad on the device you will teach with, once, while you have internet. After that it keeps working if the Wi-Fi drops.
  2. Connect to the projector or TV and switch to full screen.
  3. Walk to the back of the room and look. Write one line with the pen size and color you plan to use.
  4. Turn off sleep for the lesson: set the screen timeout to 30 minutes or more, and plug the device in.
  5. Turn off notifications, or use Do Not Disturb, so messages don’t pop up on the big screen.

Common problems and fixes

ProblemTry this
The projector shows “No signal”.Check that the cable is pushed in firmly at both ends, then choose the right input on the projector (HDMI 1, HDMI 2 or VGA). On Windows, press Windows + P again and choose Duplicate.
The picture is cut off at the edges.Look for Aspect ratio or Zoom in the projector menu and choose 16:9 or Auto. On a laptop, try a lower screen resolution, like 1280 × 720.
Everything looks tiny.Use full screen, the Thick pen and Large text. Zoom in on the board with two fingers or Ctrl and the mouse wheel.
Casting is slow or lags behind my pen.Move closer to the TV or Wi-Fi router, close other apps, or use a cable. Cables never lag.
My hand draws lines while I write on the tablet.Turn on Pen only. Your palm and fingers stop drawing, and fingers scroll and zoom instead.
The colors look washed out.Close the curtains or turn off the lights near the screen, and use the Blackboard or Greenboard.

After class

Your pages are saved on your device automatically. Use Save page as image to keep a copy or to share a page with students who were absent, for example in your class group chat or learning system.
