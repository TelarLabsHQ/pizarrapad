Noise meter

Press Start

Uses your microphone to measure loudness. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

How to use the noise meter

  1. Press Start and allow microphone access when your browser asks.
  2. Choose the level for the activity: Silent work, Partner talk or Group work. Each one allows a different amount of noise.
  3. Show it on the projector with Full screen. Green means just right, yellow means getting loud, red means too loud.
  4. If it turns red too easily (or never), adjust the Sensitivity slider. Every room and microphone is different.

Your privacy

The noise meter only measures how loud the room is, many times a second. It doesn’t record, save or send any sound, and it can’t hear words. Press Stop and the microphone turns off. You can check: your browser shows a microphone symbol while it’s on.

Making a noise meter work in your class

  • Agree on the levels together. Before you start, model what “partner talk” and “silent work” sound like, and test the meter with the class.
  • Stay positive. Point out when the class keeps it green: “Ten minutes in the green, great focus!”
  • Use a goal, not a punishment. For example, if the class stays out of red for the whole activity, they earn two minutes of free choice at the end.
  • Expect some yellow. Group work is supposed to be a bit noisy. Red is the only level that needs a reminder.
  • Calibrate in an empty room. Set the sensitivity so normal classroom background noise stays green.

Where it helps most

  • Independent reading and silent writing time.
  • Group projects and science experiments.
  • Indoor lunch or rainy-day breaks.
  • Exam rooms and libraries.

Questions

Why does it ask for the microphone? To measure loudness. Without it, the meter can’t work. Nothing is recorded.

Which devices work? Most laptops, tablets and phones with a browser like Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. A laptop near the middle of the room gives the best reading.

Is it free? Yes, with no sign-up.
