How to use the Pomodoro timer

  1. Pick a plan: the classic Pomodoro (25 minutes of focus, 5-minute breaks, 4 rounds, then a 15-minute long break), Study 50 / 10, Short 15 / 3, or My own steps.
  2. Change any number: focus time, break time, rounds, and the long break (0 for none).
  3. Press Start and put it on full screen. A chime plays between steps, the screen changes color for breaks, and the next step starts by itself.
  4. The dots show every step, so the class can see how far they are. Skip moves to the next step; the space bar starts and pauses.

What is the Pomodoro technique?

It’s a simple way to focus: work on one task for a short, fixed time, then take a real break. Short sprints make a big task feel manageable, and planned breaks keep energy up. After a few rounds, a longer break helps the brain reset. Many students find 25 minutes on, 5 minutes off easier than “study for two hours”.

Ideas for the classroom

  • Independent work: 15 minutes of focus and a 3-minute stretch break, twice, in one period.
  • Stations or centers: use My own steps with one line per station: “Station 1 10”, “Move 1”, “Station 2 10”.
  • Lesson flow: “Warm-up 5”, “Mini lesson 10”, “Group work 15”, “Share 8”, so everyone sees what’s next.
  • Exam review days and study halls: 50 / 10 for older students.
  • At home: students can use the same timer for homework and test review.

Break ideas

  • Stand, stretch and shake it out for one minute.
  • Drink water, or look out of the window and rest your eyes.
  • A quick round of the icebreaker cards, or a one-question chat with a partner.

Questions

Does it keep time if I switch tabs?

Yes. The timer counts from the clock, so it stays right even if the tab is in the background. The tab’s title shows the time left.

Is it saved?

Your plan is saved in this browser, so it’s ready next time. Nothing is sent to us.
